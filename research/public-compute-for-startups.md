# Using public and free compute for your own startup: rules and route
_Last checked: 2026-11-16_

Builds on `government-compute.md` (public compute cannot be resold; the US LLC is not an eligible EuroHPC user; paid public prices) and `demand-side.md` (Playground / Fast Lane / Large Scale overview, Serbia's National AI Platform, SAIFA). This file answers a different question: can the founder's **own** startup use free or subsidised compute, what may it do with it, and how would he get it.

## Summary

- **Direct answer: yes, but only for building, not for running.** A small company established in Montenegro or Serbia can get free GPU time on EU supercomputers through the EuroHPC AI Factories "Industrial Innovation" calls, keep what it produces and sell it [1][2]. But the rules say allocations "should not be exploited for production usage" [1]. Public compute can pay for **R&D bursts**: training or fine-tuning a model, generating a synthetic dataset, a large simulation or data-processing run. It cannot host a live product, an API or a continuous pipeline.
- **Eligibility verdict.** The **US LLC is not eligible** (the organisation must be established in an EU Member State or a country associated to Horizon Europe / Digital Europe; the US is neither) [1][2]. A **Montenegrin d.o.o. is eligible**: Montenegro is associated to both programmes [6][7], and a Montenegrin start-up (CyberCo) has won three Fast Lane allocations since late 2025 [8][9][10]. A Serbian d.o.o. is also eligible for EuroHPC. Serbia's own National AI Platform and Innovation Fund, however, have required **majority Serbian ownership** and park membership [13][17], so they probably exclude a founder who is not Serbian (unverified for 2026).
- **Best routes for this founder:** (1) EuroHPC AI Factories **Playground → Fast Lane** (free, 1–3 months, up to 50,000 GPU-hours, decision in 2–4 working days, short technical form) [2][3][4]; (2) **commercial cloud start-up credits** (AWS, Microsoft, Google, OVHcloud, Scaleway), the only free compute that may run a production service, though a bootstrapped company gets only about $1,000–$10,000 [20][21][24]; (3) **Montenegro Innovation Fund** early-stage start-up grant (up to €30,000 at 90% plus €5,000 mentoring in the 2024 round), which pays cash that can buy compute or a part-time engineer [15][16].
- **What the compute may be used for (EuroHPC AI Factories):** civilian AI work only, compliant with the EU AI Act. The work must match the approved project. You own the generated data and models, and SMEs may exploit them commercially [2]. You must still publish results "except for the parts covered by confidentiality", acknowledge EuroHPC, file a final report within 3 months, and let EuroHPC publish that report after one year [2]. Inference should be a "small proportion" of time [1]. No resale, no crypto mining, no shared logins [11].
- **The project must be AI.** The AI Factory calls cover "ethical AI, machine learning… foundation models and generative AI" [2]. Plain simulation, analytics or data processing with no AI element would fall under Benchmark/Development access (small, for code testing, not production) or Regular access (peer-reviewed, six-month evaluation, publication expected) [1].
- **How long it lasts:** 1–3 months for Playground and Fast Lane, 3–12 months for Large Scale, with **no extensions** [2]. Repeat applications are possible once the previous one ends (CyberCo got 35,000, then 15,000 GPU-hours on LUMI-G, then time on Leonardo) [8][9][10]. Popular systems fill up and close to new requests [3][4]. Public compute can cover bursts across the first one to two years. It cannot keep a business running.
- **Hard practical blockers:** the Principal Investigator must be **employed** by the applying company [2], so a d.o.o. would need to put the founder (or an engineer) on an employment contract. HPC systems are Slurm batch clusters without root, and some do not allow outbound internet from compute nodes [11][26]. A non-technical founder can get Playground with NCC help, but needs a technical partner to *use* the allocation.
- **Money:** a Montenegrin d.o.o. costs about €20 in state fees and €1 capital, and takes about 2–4 weeks with the bank account [25]. Accounting, an employment contract and payroll contributions are the real cost and could take most of the $2,000 budget (accountant to confirm).

## Details

### 1. What a startup can get, and how

**Who counts as eligible for EuroHPC.** The Terms of Reference (V3, 31 March 2026) say industry applicants (commercial companies, SMEs and start-ups) are eligible if the **organisation** is "established or located" in a Member State or a country associated to Horizon 2020 (for LUMI, MareNostrum 5, Leonardo, Vega, MeluXina, Discoverer) or to Digital Europe / Horizon Europe (for JUPITER and newer systems) [2]. Proof is a VAT number and, if available, an EU **PIC** (free registration on the Funding & Tenders portal) [2]. The **PI must have an employment contract** with the company at submission and for 3 months after the allocation ends [2]. Montenegro and Serbia have been associated to Horizon Europe since 6 December 2021 [7] and to Digital Europe since 30 June 2023 [6]. A new d.o.o. that is not VAT-registered would probably give its tax number (PIB) instead **(unverified: ask the EuroHPC helpdesk or NCC Montenegro)**.

**Success rates.** EuroHPC publishes no acceptance rates for the AI Factory calls (searched; none found). Playground and Fast Lane are not competitive: an application that passes eligibility and technical checks is granted, first come first served, while capacity lasts [2]. Large Scale is ranked. EIC Accelerator is highly selective (no rate verified here).

**Neighbouring national schemes.** Vega (Slovenia), Discoverer (Bulgaria) and Greece's Pharos/DAEDALUS are reachable for a Montenegrin company through the **EuroHPC share** (AI Factory calls), not through their national calls. Those national calls are framed around domestic researchers and companies, and Croatia's Supek has no commercial track (see `government-compute.md`). I found no national free scheme in these countries that is open to non-nationals **(unverified for each)**.

| Who owns outputs | "Generated data and models remain under the ownership of the user" [2] | You |
| Can outputs be sold | Yes (data, models, results) [2]. Selling *access to the machine* is prohibited [11]. | Yes |

Crypto exclusions and sanctions screening are typical (not checked per provider). |

**What this means for business ideas (examples only).** Good fits: a one-off or quarterly heavy job whose *output* is the product. Examples: fine-tuning a Serbian/Croatian/Bosnian language model that you then serve cheaply elsewhere; generating a synthetic dataset you license; a batch of simulations whose results you sell as a report. Poor fits: anything that must answer customers in real time, crawl the web continuously, or depend on compute every week.

### 3. Can it keep the business running in the first years?

Bluntly: **no, not on its own.**

- Each award is 1–3 months (Playground/Fast Lane) or at most 12 months (Large Scale), with no extensions or top-ups [2]. One Playground or Fast Lane award at a time per PI [2].
- Repeats are realistic for a team that uses its hours well: CyberCo got three Fast Lane awards in about a year, with NCC Montenegro's help [8][9][10]. Each one still needs a new, justified project, and "double-awarding" of a running project is not allowed [2].
- Capacity is uneven. Four GPU partitions are closed to new Fast Lane requests [4], and the AI Factory share is a fixed slice of each machine [1].
- When an allocation ends, the jobs stop and you have 30 days to move your data out [11]. Anything customer-facing must already live elsewhere.
- Realistic picture for years 1–2: free public GPU bursts for R&D; cloud credits (probably $1k–$10k unless an accelerator or VC unlocks $100k+) for hosting; and a small grant (Montenegro Innovation Fund) for cash. Revenue has to pay for production compute by the time credits run out.

### 4. The practical route for this founder

**Which entity.**
- *US LLC:* not eligible for EuroHPC or EU programmes [1][2]. It can still apply for AWS, Microsoft, Google, NVIDIA and OVHcloud credits, which have no clear country bar (unverified per provider). The LLC's current single business is the brokerage; mixing in an R&D product muddies both (see risks).
- *Montenegrin d.o.o.:* eligible for EuroHPC, EIC and FFplus, and the natural applicant for the Montenegro Innovation Fund. Cost and time: €1 minimum capital; about €20 state fees plus translations; registration decision usually within about 2 weeks; bank account another 10–13 days, with the director present in person; fully operational in about 4 weeks [25]. Annual financial statements are required, and in practice you need an accountant. Corporate tax is 9% up to €100,000 profit [25]. One consultancy puts realistic first-year costs, including professional and accounting fees, at €2,500–4,500 (third-party, unverified). The founder already lives in Montenegro, so he would not need a foreign-director permit. Whether the founder as director can also be the "employed" PI, and what the minimum payroll cost would be, is a question for the accountant.
- *Serbian company:* only worth it if the founder (or a co-founder) is a Serbian resident or citizen. That is what unlocks the National AI Platform and Innovation Fund of Serbia (majority Serbian ownership, park membership) [13][17] **(2026 rules unverified)**. Otherwise it adds cost for nothing that Montenegro does not give.

**What an application needs.** Playground and Fast Lane need an online form at access.eurohpc-ju.europa.eu, in English, covering: project description and relevance, the target system, GPU-hours requested and time plan, software stack, an ethics self-assessment, company VAT/PIC, and a PI with a professional email [2]. Fast Lane assumes "HPC-ready" users. Large Scale adds evidence that the code runs efficiently on that machine (usually from Playground), a resource plan and a convincing innovation and impact case [2]. No business plan or CVs are required for Playground and Fast Lane per the ToR (CV/team fields in the portal not checked).

**Can a non-technical founder win and use it?** Winning a Playground allocation: probably yes, with NCC Montenegro drafting help (free) [8]. Using it: no, not alone. Someone has to package the code, write Slurm job scripts, move data and debug on an unfamiliar GPU stack. The ToR also wants the PI to be "an essential participant" [2]. The realistic options are a technical co-founder with equity, a part-time contracted ML engineer paid from a grant, or a university partner (UDG or University of Montenegro, which back NCC Montenegro). A university partner would bring open-science expectations and IP terms to agree in writing.

### 5. Risks

- **State aid / de minimis.** Free EuroHPC access is an in-kind benefit. The EuroHPC Regulation frames free SME access as addressing "market failures" [1], but I found no document saying how it is counted under EU de minimis or block-exemption rules. National schemes do count HPC support as de minimis (Slovakia: €300,000 over 3 years cap) [27]. Montenegro and Serbia have their own state-aid laws, and Innovation Fund grants will almost certainly be counted. Keep a register of every subsidy received. **(Unverified; lawyer.)**
- **Conflict with the brokerage.** Using free public compute for your own company while brokering paid compute is not prohibited by any rule found. But: (a) never let a broker client's workload run on your allocation (that is resale/sublicensing [11] and "use outside approved scope"); (b) keep the R&D company and the brokerage separate legally and in accounts; (c) do not use the allocation, NCC contacts or the EuroHPC name to market the brokerage; (d) disclose to brokerage clients that you also run a company that receives public compute, if relevant.
- **Credibility risk.** Misuse is recorded against the PI and can lead to a ban [1]. A first failed or under-used allocation hurts later ones (monthly under-use monitoring) [1].
- **Data / GDPR.** Any personal data (scraped or customer data) on a public HPC system remains your responsibility. Serbia and Montenegro are outside the EU, so transfers into the EU and back need a lawful basis [11].
- **Export control and sanctions.** Civilian-use certification and embargo screening apply at onboarding [2][11]. Dual-use topics (drones, cyber-offence, surveillance) are out.
- **Publication.** Even for SMEs, a public results report and EuroHPC acknowledgement are required [2]. Do not plan a business whose value depends on nobody knowing the method.
- **Tax (flag for accountant).** The value of free compute and grants may be taxable income or need specific accounting in Montenegro. A Montenegrin d.o.o. owned by a Montenegrin tax resident alongside a US LLC raises transfer-pricing and permanent-establishment questions. Payroll for the PI adds social contributions.

## Recommended route

1. **Do not form anything yet.** First, write a one-page "compute project" for whichever idea the other agent's work selects. It should say which AI task, what output, how many GPU-hours and which data, and confirm it is a one-off batch job, not a service.
2. **Talk to NCC Montenegro** (eurocc.udg.edu.me; free). Ask: (a) can a new d.o.o. without VAT registration apply with its PIB; (b) can the director/owner be the PI; (c) which systems have Playground capacity now and allow outbound internet or staged downloads; (d) whether they can review the draft.
3. **Find the technical partner before the allocation**: a freelance ML engineer or a UDG/UCG researcher, with a short written agreement on IP ownership (company owns outputs) and pay (equity, grant-funded fee, or both).
4. **Get an accountant's quote** for a Montenegrin d.o.o. with one employee (the founder) at the minimum lawful salary. If formation plus 6 months of accounting and payroll exceeds about $1,200, wait until a grant or revenue covers it.
5. **Form the d.o.o.** (about 4 weeks, €20 fees plus professional costs) [25] and **register a PIC** on the EU Funding & Tenders portal (free) [2].
6. **Apply for Playground** (decision within 2 working days), run the test, then **Fast Lane** (up to 50,000 GPU-hours) if the test works [2]. File every final report on time.
7. **In parallel, apply for cloud credits** for anything that must run continuously: AWS Activate Founders, Microsoft for Startups, Scaleway/OVHcloud (EU hosting), NVIDIA Inception. Use the d.o.o. if it exists; otherwise the US LLC can apply for non-EU programmes.
8. **Watch for the Montenegro Innovation Fund early-stage call** (fondzainovacije.me / programifonda.me) and apply with the d.o.o. Budget the 10% co-funding [15][16].
9. **Diary dates:** FFplus third call (spring/summer 2027, verify) [19]; EIC Accelerator short proposal only once there is a prototype and traction [18].
10. **Keep it separate from the brokerage**: separate entity, bank account and branding; note it in the decision log.

## Questions for a lawyer / accountant

1. Can the founder be both the director of a Montenegrin d.o.o. and its employee (to act as EuroHPC PI)? What is the minimum monthly cost of that employment (salary, tax, contributions)?
2. Does free EuroHPC GPU time count as state aid or de minimis aid received by the d.o.o. (EU rules and Montenegro's state-aid law)? Does it reduce what the company may receive from the Montenegro Innovation Fund?
3. Is the market value of free compute or cloud credits taxable income for a Montenegrin d.o.o.? How should grants be booked?
4. If the founder owns both a US LLC (brokerage) and a Montenegrin d.o.o. (R&D product), what are the transfer-pricing, permanent-establishment and US reporting (Form 5472 / 5471-type) consequences? Should the d.o.o. be owned personally or by the LLC?
5. If a freelance engineer or university partner works on the allocation, what contract terms secure company ownership of the code, data and models?
6. If the project processes personal data on an EU supercomputer from a non-EU company, which GDPR role and transfer mechanism applies?
7. For the Serbian schemes: does "majority ownership by Serbian residents" include a Serbian citizen resident in Montenegro?
