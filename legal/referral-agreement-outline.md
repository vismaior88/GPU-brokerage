# Referral / partner agreement with suppliers — OUTLINE FOR LAWYER REVIEW
_Last checked: 2026-11-09_

> **This is an outline for lawyer review, not a final contract and not legal advice.** Do not sign or send it as a contract. In practice most suppliers will send you *their* agreement; use Part B to review it and Part A to know what to ask for. Background and sources: `/research/legal-and-contracts.md` (source numbers below refer to that file).

## Summary

- Expect to sign the supplier's paper. Your leverage is small; choose three or four points to negotiate and accept the rest.
- The points worth fighting for: **commission tail, deal-registration protection, survival of commissions after termination, and no ban on disclosed buyer fees.**
- Never accept: exclusivity, authority to bind the supplier, handling customer money, an uncapped indemnity, or a guarantee of customer payment.
- Before signing anything, check that it does not conflict with your buyer agreement (commission disclosure, buyer fee).

## Part A. Clause-by-clause outline

| # | Clause | Ideal position | Acceptable fallback | Walk away if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Parties** | Your US LLC and the supplier entity that actually invoices customers | Supplier affiliate, if it is the paying entity | The paying entity is unclear or in a sanctioned/high-risk jurisdiction |
| 2 | **Role and relationship** | Independent contractor; non-exclusive referral partner; no authority to bind, quote binding prices or sign for the supplier; supplier contracts with and bills the customer directly | Same (this is standard [1][2]) | You are described as reseller, agent with authority, or collector of payments |
| 3 | **Non-exclusivity** | Expressly non-exclusive both ways; you may refer the same customer to other suppliers | Silence on exclusivity | Any exclusivity, non-compete or "must offer us first" duty |
| 4 | **Scope of products** | All GPU/compute products, all regions, including reserved and dedicated capacity | Listed products only | Only self-serve on-demand usage when your deals are reserved contracts |
| 5 | **Deal registration** | Register by email or portal with customer legal name, domain and need; supplier accepts or rejects **in writing within 3 business days**; rejection only if the customer is an existing customer or in a documented active opportunity | 5–10 business days; rejection reasons listed | Acceptance is at "sole discretion" with no reasons and no written record |
| 6 | **Registration protection period** | 12 months from acceptance, extended automatically while negotiations are active | 6 months (Hyperbolic gives 180 days [4]), renewable on request | Under 90 days for enterprise-size deals |
| 7 | **Commission rate and base** | Percentage of net revenue **collected** from the referred customer; rate and worked example in a schedule; discounts and credits defined | Tiered rate by term or size (as ICS: 8% / 10% [7]) | Base is undefined, or paid in non-cashable credits only [3][5] |
| 8 | **Tail (duration)** | Life of the customer relationship ("evergreen"), including renewals, extensions and expansions | Full initial contract term plus first renewal; or minimum 24 months | 6 months or first transaction only, on multi-year contracts (unless the rate compensates) |
| 9 | **Renewals, upsell, affiliates of the customer** | Included | Included for 24 months | Expressly excluded and the tail is short |
| 10 | **Ban on customer-paid fees** | No restriction, or express permission to charge the customer disclosed advisory fees | Permission on written notice to supplier | Commission is forfeited if the customer pays you anything (as in DO affiliate terms [1]) **and** you plan a buyer fee on that deal |
| 11 | **Commission pass-back to customer** | Allowed if disclosed and paid to the customer company (never to an individual) | Supplier gives the discount directly instead | Treated as a prohibited "incentive" with forfeiture, and you promised the buyer a rebate |
| 12 | **Payment timing, currency, threshold** | Monthly, within 30 days of supplier receiving payment; USD wire to the LLC's bank account; no minimum | Quarterly (Hyperbolic [4]); minimum up to $100 | Payment only on request, or threshold so high small deals never pay |
| 13 | **Statements** | Per-customer statement each period: revenue collected, rate, commission | Summary statement plus detail on request | No reporting at all |
| 14 | **Audit right** | Once a year, independent accountant, supplier pays if under-payment over 5% | Right to request supporting records | (Not a walk-away point; rarely granted) |
| 15 | **Clawback** | Only for refunds, chargebacks or proven fraud; pro rata; 6–12 month look-back; by set-off against future commission only | Longer look-back, still set-off only | "Questionable" transactions at sole discretion, with a right to invoice you [1] and no limit |
| 16 | **Customer non-payment** | You bear no credit risk beyond not earning commission on unpaid amounts | Same | Any guarantee or indemnity for customer payment |
| 17 | **Changes to terms** | Written agreement of both; or 30+ days' notice and **no effect on registered or closed deals** | 30 days' notice, right to terminate, closed deals protected | Unilateral change effective immediately on existing deals [1] |
| 18 | **Term and termination** | Either party on 30 days' notice; immediate for material breach or compliance cause | 15 days [1] | Supplier-only termination rights |
| 19 | **Post-termination commissions** | Continue on all deals registered before termination for the full tail, unless terminated for your material breach | Continue 12–24 months | End at termination regardless of cause |
| 20 | **Compliance** | Mutual: export controls, sanctions, anti-bribery; supplier, as provider, runs its own customer KYC and export screening and may refuse any customer; you run your own screening and disclose red flags | One-way reps by you, if accurate | You must guarantee the customer's compliance, or supplier disclaims all screening |
| 21 | **Commission disclosure** | You may (and will) disclose to the customer that the supplier pays you, and the rate | Disclosure of the fact but not the rate | Agreement requires you to keep the commission secret from the customer |
| 22 | **Confidentiality** | Mutual; survives 2–3 years; lets you share with your lawyer/accountant and disclose commission to buyers | Supplier form NDA | Bars commission disclosure |
| 23 | **Non-circumvention / lead ownership** | Supplier will not avoid commission by contracting with a registered customer through an affiliate or another channel during the protection period | Covered implicitly by deal registration | (Not a walk-away point if 5, 6 and 19 are solid) |
| 24 | **Marketing and trademarks** | Limited licence to say "referral partner of X"; you may publish neutral comparisons | Pre-approval of materials | Bans on comparing with competitors |
| 25 | **Representations about the service** | You pass on only supplier-provided materials; supplier is responsible for their accuracy | Same | You warrant supplier performance |
| 26 | **Liability cap** | Mutual; yours capped at commissions received in the prior 12 months; no indirect damages | Supplier cap lower than yours but yours still capped | Your liability uncapped |
| 27 | **Indemnity** | Each party for its own breach of law and third-party IP claims on its materials | You indemnify only for your own misrepresentation or legal violation, within the cap (except fraud) | You indemnify for customer conduct |
| 28 | **Taxes** | Each party pays its own; LLC provides the correct IRS form (accountant to confirm which W-8/W-9 form applies) | Same | Supplier withholds without explanation — ask accountant first |
| 29 | **Governing law and disputes** | Your LLC's state or a neutral US state (Delaware/New York); negotiation step, then courts or low-cost arbitration | Supplier's law and arbitration seat (NY/AAA in DO's case [1][2]) | Law of a jurisdiction where you cannot realistically get advice |
| 30 | **Assignment / change of control** | Commission obligations bind the supplier's successor | Silence | Agreement ends automatically on supplier's acquisition with commissions lost |

## Part B. Checklist for reviewing a supplier's standard partner terms

Read the whole document plus every "Program Guide", "Program Policies" or portal page it refers to (amounts and timing are often only there [1][2]). Tick each item.

**Money**
- [ ] Commission rate, and whether it is on invoiced, collected, or net revenue
- [ ] Cash or credits? Any conversion or permanence rule (RunPod's affiliate opt-in is permanent [3])
- [ ] How long commission runs (months? first transaction only? renewals?)
- [ ] Minimum deal size or minimum number of referrals before anything is paid
- [ ] Payout schedule, minimum threshold, payment method, fees, currency
- [ ] Holding period before payout and whether it can be extended without notice [1]
- [ ] Clawback triggers, look-back, and whether they can invoice you
- [ ] Tax forms required

**Ownership of the deal**
- [ ] How a referral is registered (link, form, email, portal) and what written proof you get
- [ ] Grounds for rejecting a referral (existing customer, "active sales process" within X days [1][4])
- [ ] Time limit for the customer to sign after registration
- [ ] What happens if two partners register the same customer

**Restrictions on you**
- [ ] Exclusivity or non-compete (reject)
- [ ] Ban on receiving anything from the customer (conflicts with buyer fee)
- [ ] Ban on incentives/rebates to customers (conflicts with pass-back)
- [ ] Confidentiality wide enough to block commission disclosure to buyers
- [ ] Marketing limits (brand keywords, comparisons, required "partner" disclosure)
- [ ] Any authority-to-bind or reseller language (you want none)

**Exit**
- [ ] Notice period for each side; is it symmetrical? [2]
- [ ] Commissions after termination — for how long, and in which termination scenarios [1]
- [ ] Unilateral amendment right and whether it affects existing deals

**Risk**
- [ ] Your liability cap; their cap
- [ ] Indemnities you give
- [ ] Compliance reps — can you truthfully give each one?
- [ ] Who screens the customer for export control/sanctions (supplier must do its own)
- [ ] Governing law, forum, arbitration, costs

**Decision rule:** if three or more "walk away" items from Part A appear and the supplier will not change them, treat the supplier as a non-commission option you may still recommend to buyers under a buyer-paid fee, rather than as a commission partner.
